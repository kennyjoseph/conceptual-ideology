\subsection*{The alignment regression under keyword covariates}

The arms above run with an intercept-only design, which keeps the community-recovery
score honest but leaves $\beta$ with nothing to generalize the pins by. This arm puts
covariates back: eleven binary indicators read off the actors' profile descriptions
(occupational---\texttt{m\_media}, \texttt{m\_family}---and ideological---\texttt{m\_maga},
\texttt{m\_blm}, \texttt{m\_pronouns}, and so on), standardized and entered in $m_a$
alongside the intercept. Everything else matches the selected arm: $F_k{=}1$, 2012--2022,
white supremacy not modeled, $\mathcal{C}1$ and $\mathcal{C}2$ withheld with
$\mathcal{C}3$ retained, and the same six pinned actors. It is fit on all 62{,}045 units
with no holdout, so nothing reported here is a test---the table is a description of the
fitted alignment regression, and the model-selection evidence stays with the arms above.

Read the coefficients as logits of $\theta_a = \mathrm{softmax}(\beta' m_a + \xi_a)$:
one column per component plus the background coordinate, per standard deviation of a
standardized indicator. The regression rather than the actor draw is carrying the
alignment here---across actors, $\beta' m_a$ has standard deviation 1.52, 1.62 and 1.31
on the three columns against 0.12, 0.31 and 0.33 for $\xi_a$---so $\beta$ is close to a
multivariate regression of each actor's fitted $\mathrm{CB}-\mathrm{IS}$ log-odds on the
covariates. The last two columns give that regression directly, univariate and
multivariate, because the gap between them is what the $\beta$ column cannot show on its
own.

One covariate carries the alignment. \texttt{m\_maga} is the only large coefficient
($+1.02$ toward colorblindness, $-0.84$ away from intersectionality), with
\texttt{m\_pronouns} and \texttt{m\_blm} the substantial markers in the other direction;
\texttt{m\_conservative} and \texttt{m\_guns} point the right way but an order of
magnitude weaker, and the two occupational columns sit at zero, which is what an
occupational control should do. \texttt{m\_faith} is the one column that loads on the
\emph{background} coordinate rather than on either ideology ($+0.84$, with negative
weights on both): religious self-description marks an actor as speaking less
ideologically in this framework's sense, not as speaking for one side.

Two coefficients must not be read as absence of signal, and this is what the univariate
column is for. \texttt{m\_prolife} goes from $7.42$ alone to $0.43$ jointly and
\texttt{m\_resist} from $-1.84$ to $0.01$: all thirteen \texttt{m\_prolife} actors also
carry \texttt{m\_maga}, and thirteen of the twenty-three \texttt{m\_resist} actors carry
\texttt{m\_blm} or \texttt{m\_liberal}, so a stronger collinear column takes the credit in
the joint fit. The same attenuation, less severely, affects \texttt{m\_conservative} and
\texttt{m\_guns}. A multivariate coefficient near zero here reports redundancy given the
other ten indicators, not that the keyword fails to distinguish adherents.

Three limits bound what this table can support. First, pinned actors contribute exactly
zero gradient to $\beta$, and none of the six pins carries a covariate, so $\beta$'s
directions are identified entirely from unpinned actors' fitted memberships---the
covariates never touch the labeled anchors. Second, 321 of the 607 actors carry no
keyword at all and receive the reference row, so the regression informs about half the
corpus. Third, keyword matching has no stance channel: the words for a politics are also
the words its opponents use for it, and the residual errors are asymmetric, each landing
on an actor of the opposing side. Taken with the companion result that these covariates
do not improve whole-actor holdout while the community label does, the table is best read
as a description of which self-descriptions travel with which component in this corpus,
and not as evidence that profile text identifies alignment.
