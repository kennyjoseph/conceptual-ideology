\section{Evaluation in detail}\label{app:evaluation}

Four quantities are reported for every arm of the ladder. They answer different
questions and are not interchangeable: held-out fit asks whether the composed rates
predict expressions the model has not seen; concept-mapping AUC asks whether the
components landed on the codebook's concepts without being told them; structural
coherence asks whether each component's association mass stays inside its own support;
and the actor-share check asks whether the components are describing distinct
populations at all. This appendix states each precisely, together with what it cannot
support.

\subsection{Held-out fit}\label{app:heldout}

\paragraph{The split.} Before fitting, and independently for each actor $a$,
$\mathrm{round}(0.1\,n_a)$ of that actor's $n_a$ units are drawn without replacement and
withheld. Stratifying within actor is not cosmetic: $\mu_a$ and $\gamma_a$ are
actor-level parameters, so an unstratified draw can leave a low-volume actor with no
training units, making its parameters unestimable, or with no held-out units, making its
fit unscorable. In the reported corpus the split withholds $6{,}195$ of $62{,}045$ units
($9.98\%$). One consequence follows from the rounding: $\mathrm{round}(0.1\,n_a)=0$
whenever $n_a\le 5$, so the $63$ actors with five or fewer units contribute no held-out
units. They are still fitted, and still constrain the shared parameters, but they do not
enter the score.

\paragraph{What the training mask governs.} Everything derived from data is derived from
the training units alone: the fixed background rates $m_u$, the kernel-weighted pair
counts $A^{(\delta)}$ and their exposure $N^{(\delta)}$, and every fitted parameter. The
held-out units enter only at scoring time.

\paragraph{The score.} Units are collapsed onto the (actor, period) grid, each cell $g$
storing the number of units $n_g$ it contains and the summed extractor confidences
$X_{gu}$ per node; the reported corpus gives $2{,}796$ training cells and $1{,}317$
held-out cells. The collapse is exact rather than an approximation, since the Poisson
log-likelihood of units sharing a rate depends on the data only through that count and
that sum. Writing $\hat z_{gu}$ for the fitted log rate,
\[
  \mathrm{HO}\;=\;\sum_{g\in\text{test}}\sum_u\Big[\,n_g\,e^{\hat z_{gu}}
  \;-\;X_{gu}\,\hat z_{gu}\Big],
\]
the Poisson negative log-likelihood without the data-only factorial term. Lower is
better. No marginalisation is required, and none is performed: the model has no free
per-unit parameter, so a held-out unit is scored under rates fitted entirely from other
units.

\paragraph{What it tests.} This is a \emph{within-actor} holdout. Each actor's $\mu_a$
and $\theta_a$ are estimated from their own training units, so the question is whether
the profiles and the composed rate generalise to that actor's unseen expressions---not
whether the model can place an actor it has never encountered.

\paragraph{Three cautions.} First, $\mathrm{HO}$ is a sum rather than a mean, so it is
comparable only across arms fitted on an identical split; every arm reported here shares
one split (same seed, same units). Second, its scale is easy to over-read: the reported
values correspond to $\approx 3.50$ nats per held-out unit, or $\approx 0.031$ per
(unit, node) cell, so a gap of a few hundred nats between arms is a difference of
$\sim\!0.03$ nats per unit. Third, and decisively for model selection,
$\mathrm{HO}$ is not free of the parameter count: each added facet gives every actor a
further logit. In our results its best three values all belong to arms in which one
component has absorbed the corpus (Section~\ref{app:share}), which is why it is never
read alone.

\paragraph{Why not whole-actor holdout.} Section~\ref{sec:validation} makes whole-actor
holdout (V7) the criterion for the facet count, and that is right when actor identity is
in the design. It is the wrong instrument here, for a structural rather than a
statistical reason: V7 scores a held-out actor with their parameters at the prior and
their membership from $\beta^\top m_a$ alone, so with an intercept-only design
\emph{every} held-out actor receives an identical membership. The model cannot tell them
apart, pins cannot help---a held-out actor is by definition unpinned---and V7 therefore
cannot reward a facet structure for capturing actor variation. We accordingly select on
held-out units and report the facet question as unresolved rather than settled.

\subsection{Concept-mapping AUC}\label{app:auc}

Because $\CC1$ is withheld during fitting, the codebook's own assignment of concepts to
ideologies is available as an evaluation set: nothing in the fit has seen it. Let
$G_k\subseteq\{1,\dots,K\}$ collect the concepts the codebook assigns to ideology $k$.
Ranking all $K$ concepts by the fitted profile $\max_f\eta_{k,f,u}$, we report the
rank-based (Mann--Whitney) area under the ROC curve,
\[
  \mathrm{AUC}_k=\frac{1}{|G_k|\,|G_k^{c}|}
  \sum_{u\in G_k}\ \sum_{u'\notin G_k}
  \Big[\1\{\eta_{k,u}>\eta_{k,u'}\}+\tfrac12\,\1\{\eta_{k,u}=\eta_{k,u'}\}\Big],
\]
with $0.5$ chance and ties taking average ranks. Two properties recommend it here. It is
scored as multi-label retrieval rather than as a classification, which the codebook
requires: $22$ of the $112$ concepts carry more than one ideology, and no concept is
assigned to both colour-blindness and intersectionality. And it is free of where the
support threshold falls, unlike the precision and recall of $S_k$, which we report beside
it in Table~\ref{tab:ladder-full} because $S_k$ is the set the coherence statistic and
the edge tables actually use. We report the macro average
$\overline{\mathrm{AUC}}=|\mathcal{K}|^{-1}\sum_{k\in\mathcal{K}}\mathrm{AUC}_k$ over the
ideologies the codebook labels; a free component has no $G_k$ and is excluded rather than
scored against a mapping it was never given.

\subsection{Structural coherence and its null}\label{app:coherence}

\paragraph{The statistic.} For ideology $k$ with support
$S_k=\{u:\max_f\eta_{k,f,u}>0\}$,
\[
  C_k=\frac{\sum_{(u,u')\in\PP,\;u\in S_k,\;u'\in S_k}W_{k,uu'}}
           {\sum_{(u,u')\in\PP,\;u\in S_k\,\oplus\,u'\in S_k}W_{k,uu'}},
\]
where $\oplus$ is exclusive or, so the denominator collects exactly the pairs with one
endpoint inside the support and one outside. $C_k>1$ says the ideology's concepts relate
more strongly to each other than across its own boundary. It is computed on a fit with
the $\CC6$ sparsity gate \emph{off}, so that it is able to fail; the gate is only ever
switched on afterwards.

\paragraph{Relation to conductance.} $C_k$ is not a new statistic, and it is worth
naming its counterpart in the network-clustering literature before reporting it. Writing
$m_{S}$ for the internal edge weight of a set $S$ and $c_{S}$ for its cut,
$C_k=m_{S_k}/c_{S_k}$ is one-to-one with the weighted \emph{conductance} of $S_k$:
\[
  \phi(S_k)=\frac{c_{S_k}}{2m_{S_k}+c_{S_k}}=\frac{1}{1+2C_k},
  \qquad C_k=\frac{1-\phi(S_k)}{2\phi(S_k)} .
\]
The two therefore order supports identically and either may be reported; we use the ratio
because its unit is the one the framework's definition of an ideology asserts---$C_k=1$
is as much association weight inside the support as crossing its boundary. In the
selected arm colour-blindness has $m_{S}=86.6$, $c_{S}=59.9$, hence $C_k=1.45$ and
$\phi=0.257$; intersectionality has $m_{S}=138.6$, $c_{S}=31.6$, hence $C_k=4.38$ and
$\phi=0.102$. The weaker condition $2m_{S}>c_{S}$, i.e.\ $C_k>1/2$, is
Radicchi et al.'s \emph{weak community}; the criterion used here is the stricter
$C_k>1$. The permutation null below plays the role modularity's configuration null plays
for internal density, with one difference that matters in this setting: it preserves the
\emph{type} composition of the support as well as its degree profile, because $\CC5$
makes certain rung pairs structurally incapable of carrying mass.

\paragraph{Why a null is required.} $C_k$ is not interpretable on its own. $W$'s mass
concentrates on a few high-degree nodes, so a support assembled from hubs scores well
whatever it contains, and a support drawn from one rung scores differently from one
spread across rungs, since the ladder constraint governs which pairs may carry mass at
all. Matching on size alone would make almost any support look coherent.

\paragraph{The null.} Each of $200$ null supports is drawn to match $S_k$ in size
\emph{and} composition. All $K$ concepts are bucketed by a two-part stratum: concept type
($V/A/B/R$), and within type the quartile of weighted degree in $W_k$. A draw then takes
from each bucket exactly as many concepts as $S_k$ contains, sampled without replacement,
and $C_k$ is recomputed with that support substituted into the \emph{same} fitted $W_k$.
For colour-blindness in the selected arm, the support of $58$ decomposes as $8$ values,
$18$ attitudes, $16$ beliefs and $16$ rationalisations, with the quartile counts of
Table~\ref{tab:ladder-full}'s companion; every null support reproduces those counts
exactly. The resulting null has mean $0.716$ and standard deviation $0.154$ against an
observed $C_k=1.445$.

\paragraph{The $p$-value.} Non-finite draws (a support with zero boundary mass) are
discarded before comparison, so
\[
  p=\frac{\#\{C_k^{\text{null}}\ge C_k\}}{\#\{\text{finite }C_k^{\text{null}}\}} .
\]
With $200$ permutations the smallest attainable value is $.005$; a reported $p<.005$
means no admissible null draw matched the observed statistic.

\paragraph{Undefined cases.} Two failures are reported rather than smoothed over. If a
component has positive inside mass and \emph{exactly zero} boundary mass, $C_k$ is
infinite and the null admits no comparison. If it has no association mass at all,
$C_k$ is undefined; we print a dash. The second is the stronger failure, and it is what
white supremacy exhibits at $F_k\ge2$: the component acquires no edges, so there is no
structure whose coherence could be assessed.

\paragraph{What the test does not do.} The permutation moves the \emph{support}, holding
$W_k$ fixed. It therefore asks whether the fitted support is better placed than a matched
random support on the same network---not whether the network itself carries more
structure than chance. That second question is the zero-ideology null's
(Section~\ref{sec:inference}).

\paragraph{A limitation of the matching.} Under a sparse $W$, degree ties at zero
collapse the quartile bins. In the selected arm all $36$ rationalisations fall in a
single degree stratum, because most have degree exactly zero and every tie lands at the
same bin edge; the same happens partially in the belief rung. Degree matching is
therefore strongest where the network is dense and weakest exactly where it is sparse---
and the rationalisation rung is both the sparsest and the source of $16$ of
colour-blindness's $58$ support nodes.

\subsection{The actor-share check}\label{app:share}

The three quantities above can all improve while the model ceases to describe a
population. We therefore report, for each component, the percentage of actors whose
ideology margin $\vartheta_{a,k}$ is largest on it. This uses only the model's own
assignment---no external labels---and it is the check that catches degeneracy: a
component that has absorbed the corpus shows a share near $100\%$, and one that has been
emptied shows a share of $0\%$. A share printed as $0\%$ is in fact three actors, namely
that component's own pinned actors held at their fixed membership; components do not
shrink gradually, they empty out to the supervision.

Two readings in the main table depend on it. Held-out fit's three best values belong to
arms with a component at $99\%$ or above, so the criterion prefers collapse. And every
arm that models white supremacy has a component at $0\%$: white supremacy itself at
$F_k\ge2$, and colour-blindness at $F_k=1$, where white supremacy takes the right-hand
position ($28\%$ of actors) and leaves colour-blindness with only its pins. We also
report the smallest effective number of actors on any facet, an inverse-Simpson count
$1/\sum_a(\theta_{a,kf}/\sum_{a'}\theta_{a',kf})^2$, which catches the complementary
failure: a component carried by a handful of accounts rather than by none. It falls to
$4.4$ in the white-supremacy arms against $364$--$514$ elsewhere.
